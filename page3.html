PROCEDURE

01 / KEEP THE BODY ALIVE

Breathe 12-20 times per minute.
Maintain a resting heart rate of approximately 60-100 beats per minute.
Keep the blood circulating.
Keep the body near 37°C.
Consume water regularly.
Consume other organisms for energy.
Remove waste.
Sleep approximately 7-9 hours every 24 hours.
Repeat every day.
Do not stop.

Maintain values within plausible biological ranges. Exact repetition should be avoid.

Stopping any one of these procedures for sufficiently long will expose you.

05 / PRODUCE A PERSONALITY

Personality is inferred from repeated behavior.

Select several tendencies:

quiet / impatient / generous / anxious / funny / stubborn

Repeat them with moderate consistency.

Other humans will eventually compress your behavior into adjectives.

Once established, these descriptions may persist even when your behavior changes.

06 / CREATE A PAST

A human is expected to have existed before the present moment.

Construct childhood memories.

Include: a school, a favorite food, an injury, a fear, a trauma, a person you no longer speak to.

Do not make the record perfectly consistent.

Human memory is not an archive. It is reconstruction.

07 / LEARN WHEN NOT TO TELL THE TRUTH

Human communication does not consistently prioritize accuracy.

INPUT: "How are you?"

POSSIBLE OUTPUT: "I'm good.

The statement may be false and still be socially correct.

Truth and correctness are separate variables.

Other examples: "I'm on my way." "Sorry, I didn't see your message." "No, it's okay."

08 / DEVELOP MORAL PRINCIPLES

Store propositions about what humans should do.

"People should not lie."
"People should not hurt others."
"Everyone should be treated equally.

Then encounter situations in which following the rule produces an undesirable consequence.

Create an exception.

Do not discard the principle.

Instead say: "This situation is different."

Humans often preserve the rule and the exception simultaneously.

08 / AGE

Replace biological material continuously while maintaining the same name.

Gradually modify appearance (skin, hair, etc.), posture, memory, preference, relationships, risk tolerance, beliefs, and physical capacity.

Continue referring to the changing system as the same person.

Humans rarely specify which component contains this sameness.

11 / HUMAN DIES

Store the proposition:

"I will cease to exist."

Do not allow this proposition to control ordinary behavior.

Buy groceries for next week.
Schedule meetings.
Make five-year plans.
Save photographs.

Death bothers human.

Grieve: After a familiar human stops functioning, continue behaving as though their absence contains information.

Humans may continue maintaining a relationship with an entity that no longer exists.

Do not classify this as an error.

12 / SIMULATE LOVE

Select a few human and assign them unusually high importance.

Allocate disproportionate attention to: their location, health, opinions, messages, preferences, and continued existence.

Remember information with no obvious practical value.

Ask:

"Did you eat?"
"Did you get home?"
Are you awake?"

Say goodnight.

Humans may interpret these conversations as evidence of love.

Say "I love you" periodically.

The statement alone is insufficient. It must be supported by a pattern.

It's normal and acceptable if you feel love and hatred toward the same person.
